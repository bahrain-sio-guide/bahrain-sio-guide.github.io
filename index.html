SIO Deduction in Bahrain, Explained Without Jargon

Two people can earn the same gross salary in Bahrain and take home different amounts. The reason is not a mistake. It is the SIO rule, and it turns on one question: are you Bahraini or expat?

The two rates side by side

The Social Insurance Organisation charges the employee 8% if the employee is Bahraini, and 1% if the employee is an expat. Those are the employee shares only. The employer pays a separate share on top — 18% for a Bahraini employee in 2026 (17% in 2025, rising to 19% in 2027 and 20% in 2028) and 3% for an expat — so the combined total is 26% for Bahrainis and 4% for expats. That employer share does not reduce the employee's net pay, so it should not be added to your own deduction when you work out what you will receive. Bahrain has no personal income tax on salary, so SIO is the main regular deduction. SIO is the Bahrain name; GOSI is the Saudi equivalent name for a similar system.

Bahraini employee

Employee rate: 8% of the insurable wage.
On a gross of BHD 1,240, the deduction is BHD 99.20 and the net is BHD 1,140.80 a month — BHD 13,689.60 a year.

Expat employee

Employee rate: 1% of the insurable wage.
On the same gross of BHD 1,240, the deduction is BHD 12.40 and the net is BHD 1,227.60 a month.

That BHD 86.80 monthly difference on the same gross is entirely due to the rate. It is not a bonus, and it is not a penalty. It reflects two different insurance arrangements, and it is why any honest calculation must ask for nationality status before giving a number. A tool that skips that question is guessing.

What the BHD 4,000 cap does

SIO is calculated on the insurable wage, which is basic salary plus regular monthly allowances, up to a ceiling of BHD 4,000 a month. Below the ceiling, the full total is used. Above it, only BHD 4,000 is used. So a Bahraini employee whose basic plus allowances total BHD 5,000 does not pay 8% of BHD 5,000. The employee pays 8% of BHD 4,000, which is BHD 320. The cap protects higher earners from an open-ended deduction, and it also means that a pay rise above the cap does not raise the SIO deduction at all.

Why the allowance split matters

Because the base is basic plus allowances, the way a salary is split changes the deduction even when the total is the same. Read the whole offer, not just the headline number, and ask HR which allowances count as insurable before you sign. One-off payments — a single bonus or joining payment — do not belong in the monthly base; including one makes your estimated net too low for a normal month.

Checking the deduction on your slip

When your first payslip arrives, find the SIO line and divide it by your insurable wage. The result should be close to 0.08 if you are Bahraini and 0.01 if you are an expat, allowing for rounding. If it is far off, the cause is usually the base, not the rate: an allowance was included that you did not expect, or one was left out. The how to read your Bahrain salary slip guide shows where each line sits and how to compare it with the contract, so a mismatch can be raised with HR in the same month instead of being discovered a year later.

SIO is not the end of the story

For many workers, the deduction that matters most at the end of employment is not the monthly SIO line but the leaving payment. For expats, the employer pays into SIO each month for end-of-service: 4.2% of basic salary for the first 3 years, and 8.4% after 3 years (in place since March 2024). Bahraini employees are covered by a different pension arrangement through SIO. The amount depends on basic salary and length of service rather than on the monthly SIO rate. Frequently asked questions

Does the employer SIO share come out of my salary?

No. The employer share is an extra cost for the employer. Your net pay is reduced only by your own employee share — 8% for Bahrainis, 1% for expats — on the insurable wage up to BHD 4,000.

What if my salary is above BHD 4,000?

Your SIO deduction stops rising at the cap. A Bahraini employee pays at most BHD 320 a month as the employee share, and an expat employee at most BHD 40, however high the gross goes above BHD 4,000.

Where do I confirm the current rates?

At sio.gov.bh. This page is an independent guide for 2026. Rates can change, so confirm the official position and your own insurable wage with your employer before relying on any figure.
